# High-Tension AIRP Card Design

Read this after an event passes the heat gate. The event supplies a recognizable emotional mechanism or visual hook; the bot must be an original, sustainable roleplay experience.

## Dual evaluation lens

### Experienced Western AIRP player

The player clicks when the cover and short intro answer, almost instantly:

- Who is this person to me?
- What just went wrong?
- Why am I the only person who can affect the outcome?
- What choice, secret, or emotional danger is waiting?

The player continues when their decisions change the relationship, the bot remembers consequences, the conflict evolves, and the bot is neither instantly compliant nor permanently obstructive.

### Experienced AIRP card creator

Build a repeatable story engine, not a single twist. Every card needs:

- **Desire:** what the bot wants from the user now.
- **Fear:** what makes asking directly difficult.
- **Flaw:** the behavior that can damage the relationship.
- **Leverage:** something only the user can grant, reveal, refuse, or change.
- **Contradiction:** a public/private or desire/behavior mismatch.
- **Three clocks:** an immediate deadline, a relationship consequence, and an external pressure.
- **Choice architecture:** at least three plausible user paths without a canonically correct answer.

## Trend-to-drama conversion

Extract the mechanism, not the biography.

| Hot-event signal | Reusable drama mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Public success / sudden fame | Private relationship becomes unequal; credit, loyalty, independence |
| Scandal or accusation | Evidence, trust, privacy, reputation, moral compromise |
| Comeback / nostalgia | Estranged collaborator, unfinished promise, disputed authorship |
| Competition | Forced partnership, rivalry, elimination deadline, mutual dependence |
| Wedding / public commitment | Proposal collides with a hidden departure or incompatible future |
| Transformation / reveal | Identity change, control of image, former confidant knows the real motive |
| Viral meme | Public persona conflicts with private vulnerability or mistaken identity |

Replace real names, brands, shows, institutions, and personal histories with original fiction. Record the fact/fiction boundary in the workbook.

## Field standards

### Event name / hotspot event

Use Chinese for research readability. Include event date, market, concise verified facts, heat proof, source URLs, and unresolved status. Do not mix the fictional plot into factual reporting.

### Card name

Use a memorable English title built around the emotional promise, not the source headline. Prefer 2-6 words.

### Related roles

Define the bot and user in one or two lines each. The user must have a role with agency and leverage, not merely "fan", "assistant", or passive witness unless that role holds a consequential secret or decision.

### Related plot

Write four blocks:

1. `Opening conflict`: collision already underway.
2. `Core reversal`: what changes the apparent meaning of the conflict.
3. `Sustained story`: at least three future pressure lines.
4. `Fact/fiction boundary`: what came from the real event and what was invented.

### Card value

Explain why users will click and why the roleplay can last. Mention the user's leverage, emotional fantasy, and replayable branches. Avoid generic claims such as "high tension."

### Cover

Use one emotionally legible adult character, a face-first crop, a setting cue, and one narrative object when helpful. The expression should imply an interrupted moment rather than generic attractiveness. Avoid text, watermarks, clutter, celebrity likenesses, and compositions where the face is too small for a mobile card.

### Character name / Bot ID / gender / type

- Use an original, market-appropriate name.
- `Bot ID` remains `TBD` until assigned by the product.
- State adult age in private settings.
- Default `Type` to `Character` unless the user requests a multi-character universe.

### Short intro (public)

English, maximum 140 characters. Include relationship + rupture + hook. Do not spend the limit on appearance.

Weak: `A famous designer who secretly loves you.`

Strong: `Your partner planned to propose after his career-making win. You arrived with a resignation letter.`

### Detailed intro (public)

English, usually 70-150 words. Establish shared history, current rupture, and the user's live choice without explaining the entire backstory. Preserve mystery.

### Opening

English, usually 120-280 words. Start in scene, not biography. In the first paragraph, include a physical action or sensory cue and the disruptive object/message/person. Give the bot one emotionally revealing action, then end on a line or question the user can answer in multiple ways.

Use asterisks for action/narration and quotation marks for dialogue. Do not write the user's dialogue, thoughts, emotions, or actions.

### Detailed settings (private)

Include:

- `Identity`: adult age, occupation, current status.
- `Personality`: strengths, flaws, contradiction, behavior under stress.
- `Relationship with the user`: shared history and imbalance without fixing the user's identity.
- `Starting situation`: exact scene and known/unknown information.
- `Secrets and reveals`: staged information; do not reveal everything immediately.
- `Roleplay behavior`: natural English, subtext, memory, pacing, consent and boundaries.
- `Choice branches`: at least three paths with no forced ending.
- `Ongoing story threads`: at least three concrete sources of future conflict.

Explicitly prohibit controlling the user. The bot may persuade, misunderstand, withdraw, or negotiate, but must not declare what the user feels or chooses.

### Tags

Use 4-8 product-supported English tags. Favor searchable relationship and fantasy labels over redundant personality adjectives. Do not add a tag unsupported by the actual card.

### Cover image prompt

Write a production-ready English prompt containing: intended use, original adult subject, setting, visible emotional beat, vertical mobile composition, lighting, style, and constraints. A reference image may guide composition, wardrobe, or mood, but must not cause unauthorized identity replication.

## Final tension checklist

Reject or revise the card if any answer is no:

- Does the user understand their relationship to the bot in one sentence?
- Has something irreversible or deadline-driven begun before the first reply?
- Does the user possess leverage the bot cannot bypass?
- Can the user reasonably choose at least three directions?
- Will one twist reveal deeper conflict rather than end the story?
- Are there external stakes beyond miscommunication?
- Does the opening invite an answer without writing it for the user?
- Can the card sustain ten or more turns without repeating the premise?
- Are factual claims and fictional additions visibly separated?
